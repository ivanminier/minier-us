# Trinity Peaks building program: 10 questions for Ivan (6 Nov 2026)

Each question has my **recommended answer**, the reason, and the alternatives. **Nothing is final until you confirm or change it.** Reply by number, for example "1 yes; 2 B; 4 swap them".

Sources: your brief (`Trinity_Peaks_Floorplans.pdf`), your 6 Nov chat corrections, your renderings and overhead, Trinity v1.3, and the University Heights and CCRH references (typology only).

---

**1. Which building is this?**
- **Recommended: Trinity Peaks.** The PDF is titled "Trinity Peaks Floorplans". The renderings show Northside Dining in the base, and v1.3 places Northside Dining inside Peaks. The brief's 6 stories plus a loft matches Peaks' "no more than six floors to the eye plus a hidden level".
- Alternative: Trinity Rise, if the renderings were meant for Rise. That would move Northside Dining and conflict with v1.3.

**2. How many RA rooms per floor?**
- **Recommended: 3 RA rooms per floor in *each* building** (6 per floor across North and South). Your chat correction (3) replaces the brief (2). One RA is the 9th bedroom in the front block; the other two sit one in each tower.
- Check against v1.3: 3 RAs × 2 buildings × floors 2–6 = **30 RA rooms**, close to v1.3's "about 36". The remainder could be on floor 1 or the loft level if student rooms are there.
- Alternatives: (B) 3 per floor across both buildings; (C) 3 per floor on floors 2–5 and fewer on the shorter floor 6.

**3. Where is the "front"?**
- **Recommended: the side facing the dining entrance and lobbies, where the ground floor is partly buried.** The 8 Jack-and-Jill singles plus the front RA sit at the head of each building's main tower, directly above its lobby. Residents pass the RA and the amenity stack on the way in.
- Alternative: the street-facing end of each tower (Colchester Avenue side).
- **Please confirm or sketch.** This sets where the main stairs, elevators, and amenity stack go.

**4. Which towers are "middle" and which are "outer"?**
- **Recommended: "middle" = the two towers facing each other across the central courtyard and glass bridges; "outer" = the two towers facing the site edges.** Narrow communal-style rooms with pod bathrooms then cluster near the bridges, courtyard, and shared amenities, where social life is. Larger rooms with attached bathrooms get the quieter edge views and fit the premium tier.
- Also tell me which tower in each building is **straight** and which is **L-shaped**. Your brief says each building has one of each, but the overhead doesn't make it clear.
- Alternative: the opposite assignment (premium rooms facing the courtyard).

**5. What are the end-of-floor blocks on floors 2–5?**
- **Recommended: a 4-person suite of 4 private bedrooms around a living room, with 2 bathrooms and no kitchen,** at each tower end. These become the premium second-year option, about 900–1,100 sf.
- Alternatives: (B) 2 doubles + living room + 1–2 baths (more beds, lower price); (C) a 6-person version (2 doubles + 2 singles + living room); (D) a mix of B and C.

**6. "Limited large triples"?**
- **Recommended: allow a small number of *designed* triples:** generous rooms (about 260–300 sf), chosen by students at a lower rate (UVM already prices "Private Triple" and "Suite Triple" rooms), **capped at about 5% of beds** and never used as overflow. This keeps faith with v1.3's "no *forced* triples".
- The public proposal wording stays unchanged unless you later decide to update it through its facts file.
- Alternative: no triples at all.

**7. What goes on floor 1?**
- **Recommended: in each building, 2 live-in staff apartments** (for example, a residence director and a faculty-in-residence, the "teacher apartments") = **4 total**, matching your brief's "4 live-in staff apartments" and v1.3's 3–5. The rest of floor 1: residential life offices, meeting rooms, the lower half of the two-storey amenity stack, and student rooms if space remains.
- Alternatives: (B) floor 1 *only* staff and faculty apartments, with more of them (tell me how many); (C) more faculty apartments in addition to the 4 staff.

**8. Bed target**
- **Recommended: keep v1.3's 1,450–1,600 beds across both buildings as the target, and let the drawings report what actually fits.** If the design lands outside the range, record that as a finding rather than adjusting rooms to hit a number.
- Alternative: set a per-building target (for example 725–800 each).

**9. Height: two tracks** *(you already said yes)*
- **Preferred design (your vision):** ground + 6 + loft, about 85–95 ft. **Requires a zoning amendment** (the adopted overlay has a 55-ft absolute ceiling, as reported); the 2022 80-ft recommendation was tabled. Presented as your preferred concept, labelled with what would need approval.
- **Compliant alternative (≤55 ft):** same room types and program logic, fewer floors, larger footprint. Shows the bed and open-space cost of compliance.
- A third, **design-dependent** case (using the buried front and roof form) only counts after a planner confirms how the city measures height.

**10. UVM reference data in public files**
- **Recommended: publish no numbers derived from the UVM drawings.** Public pages would say only "informed by University Heights and CCRH typologies". Rounded figures stay in private notes. The clean push candidate already removes room-level data. Rounded notes remain on the Peaks page and in the PDF; I'd strip those too before any push.
